# Information Product Review: Audio Annotation and DITA/XML

## Project overview

A real-world case study documenting my review and restructuring of an audio-visual annotation information product within an AI data workflow.

Working as an Information Product Reviewer, I assessed material produced by a subject matter expert against established methodology guidelines. The review identified problems with metadata specificity, classification, descriptive consistency and the separation of audio and visual information.

The work required substantial structural reorganisation rather than isolated wording changes.

## The challenge

The original product grouped 31 data entries into three broad sound tracks. These categories did not adequately distinguish the sound types represented, and some entries were inconsistently classified.

The challenge was to improve two interconnected information layers: the individual sound descriptions and the metadata defining how those descriptions were organised.

It was also necessary to prevent visual observations from appearing in fields intended exclusively for audio information.

## My approach

- **Requirements and feedback analysis:** Organised evaluation findings into metadata, data-entry and visual-description issues.
- **Taxonomy redesign:** Replaced three broad tracks with eleven more specific sound categories.
- **Metadata development:** Refined labels and descriptions to distinguish meaningful sound characteristics.
- **Classification:** Reviewed and corrected relationships between individual entries and their assigned tracks.
- **Technical editing:** Standardised terminology and descriptions using identifiable audio characteristics.
- **Information separation:** Kept audio and visual information within their appropriate fields.
- **Quality assurance:** Checked the revised structure and descriptions against the methodology guidelines.

## Results and evidence

The reworked information product comprised **11 tracks and 35 data entries**. According to the case study, it progressed to randomised quality control with one edge-case terminology adjustment and no further structural rebuild required.

The case study also reports a personal compliance grade of **4.6/5**, compared with a team average of **4.3/5**. This is presented as a broader quality record, not as a score assigned specifically to this single project.

## Structured authoring demonstration

The portfolio also provides a DITA/XML version of the case study, including a DITA map, structured topics, tables, cross-references and supporting images.

The DITA source was tested using **DITA Open Toolkit 4.4.1**, successfully generating HTML5 output.

This demonstrates structured technical authoring alongside the original information-product review work.

## Skills demonstrated

Information architecture, taxonomy design, metadata writing, classification, requirements analysis, technical editing, quality assurance, complex-content restructuring, DITA/XML and structured authoring.
