# Before You Paste It Into AI

**AI safety microlearning | Instructional design and multimedia production**

A short workplace learning experience introducing a practical three-step check before sharing information with an AI tool: **Pause → Check → Decide**. The scenario uses fictional sensitive information to demonstrate why employees should check both the content they plan to share and whether the AI tool is approved for that use.

## Learning goal

Help learners recognise personal, confidential and non-public information; pause before submitting it to AI; and decide whether to remove details, rephrase the request, use an approved method or stop.

## Development and editing process

1. **Plan the instruction.** Develop the workplace scenario and the Pause → Check → Decide sequence; map the content in a visual storyboard.
2. **Write the narration script.** Structure the spoken explanation around the learning goal and the three-step decision framework.
3. **Produce the audio.** Select a warm British-English female voice and generate the standalone recording using ElevenLabs.
4. **Prototype the video.** Create an initial narrated workplace sequence to test pacing, text placement, continuity and instructional clarity.
5. **Improve visual storytelling.** Keep the same female presenter; show a fictional sensitive draft and a revised prompt within a laptop-based AI interface. Removing identifying details is not, by itself, permission to use an unapproved tool.
6. **Develop the speaking presenter.** Use Creatify Aurora through ElevenLabs to generate an avatar-led version and integrate it with the existing workplace demonstrations.
7. **Review and refine.** Trim the unwanted opening sound, revise text placement, animate the Pause → Check → Decide emphasis and test small narration-timing adjustments. The selected integrated version uses a 165 ms audio delay.

## Key editing decisions and rationale

- **Move from a slide-like presentation to workplace storytelling.** The first narrated prototype helped test the structure, but the visual treatment felt too much like a sequence of PowerPoint slides. A speaking presenter and realistic office scenes were chosen to make the learning experience more conversational and situated in everyday work.
- **Keep one recognisable presenter.** The same woman appears across the workplace scenes and avatar-led footage to provide continuity and avoid distracting character changes. The presenter was framed and edited to keep instructional text clear of her face.
- **Prioritise visual hierarchy over large text panels.** Text was repositioned to support a natural reading path without obscuring the subject. On-screen wording was reduced where the visuals could carry the message more effectively.
- **Show the decision, not just explain it.** Two laptop views illustrate a fictional draft containing personal and confidential details and a revised AI prompt with those details removed. The AI interface appears on the computer screen, rather than as a separate slide. The revised example also retains the warning that removing details does not make an unapproved AI tool acceptable.
- **Synchronise the three-step framework with the narration.** The turquoise line moves above **Pause**, **Check** and **Decide** as each word is spoken, reinforcing the sequence at the moment the learner hears it.
- **Preserve the preferred narration.** The warm British-English voice was selected after reviewing alternatives. An unintended opening sound was trimmed for the video while the original MP3 was retained as evidence of the separate audio-production stage.
- **Choose a restrained presenter performance.** After testing an avatar with excessive hand and arm movement, the full-length generation was directed toward a steadier camera, smaller gestures and more natural facial expression. The approved presenter footage was retained as the basis for the final edit.
- **Make a deliberate lip-sync trade-off.** Specific mismatches were reviewed, and global audio offsets of 150, 165 and 200 milliseconds were compared. The **165 ms** version was selected as the best available edit. Some inaccurate mouth shapes remain; further timing shifts alone would not reliably correct them, so the existing footage was retained rather than repeatedly regenerating the entire video.

## Tools and production notes

The project used an **AI-assisted workflow**. ElevenLabs was used for voice generation and Creatify Aurora for speaking-avatar video generation. Video editing and compositing also used programmatic tools, including FFmpeg and Python. Generated workplace imagery and the presenter are illustrative, not footage of an actual employee or real confidential documents.

**Quality note:** The speaking-avatar version has some remaining lip-shape/synchronisation imperfections. It is retained as the selected portfolio iteration to demonstrate review decisions and practical trade-offs rather than presented as flawless lip-sync.

**Privacy and safety:** Names, organisations, contact details and project figures shown in demonstrations are fictional examples. Always follow your organisation's information-handling and approved-tool policies.
