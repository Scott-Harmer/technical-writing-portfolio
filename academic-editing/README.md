# Academic Editing: Doctoral Thesis

## Project overview

An anonymised academic editing sample drawn from an ongoing, four-year collaboration with a doctoral researcher writing a three-paper thesis on corporate governance and boards in Russian private companies. The wider collaboration is ongoing, with completion planned for November 2026.

The work involves substantive editing of doctoral-level English, including argument clarity, cohesion, terminology consistency, APA 7 citation checks and constructive queries to the author.

## The editorial challenge

The central challenge is to strengthen the clarity and precision of complex academic writing without changing the researcher's intended meaning, disciplinary argument or ownership of the work.

Editing also requires judgement about which issues can be resolved in the text and which require author confirmation, particularly where a quotation, citation or supporting source needs checking.

## My approach and skills demonstrated

- **Substantive academic editing:** Improving clarity, cohesion and the flow of complex arguments.
- **Terminology and consistency:** Maintaining precise, consistent language across related research material.
- **Citation quality assurance:** Identifying APA 7 and source-support questions without presenting unverified references as resolved.
- **Author collaboration:** Using constructive comments to distinguish editorial recommendations from questions requiring the researcher's decision.
- **Editorial ethics:** Preserving the author's academic voice, meaning and responsibility for the research.

## About the portfolio sample

The three-paragraph example was **developed collaboratively for portfolio demonstration** using thesis material. It should not be interpreted as a verbatim record of historical editing changes.

Outstanding source queries remain visible rather than being presented as resolved. The material is anonymised and shared with the author's permission.
